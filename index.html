Hardware-identification labels

LABEL WHAT IT MEANS
OFFICIAL MODEL / CODE PORT Softube or the hardware partner explicitly names the modeled device or codebase.
UNLICENSED ID The interface, date, circuit behavior, and public technical reporting identify the donor with high confidence, but Softube does not print the trademark on the product.
ARCHETYPE / LINEAGE The processor draws from a recognizable design family; it should not be treated as a component-for-component claim.
SOFTUBE ORIGINAL A purpose-built design or combination rather than a declared replica.

Two rules that improve almost every plug-in

  1. Level-match bypass. Analog-modeled processors often add gain, and a 0.5 dB advantage can overwhelm a fair tonal judgment.

  2. Choose by job before flavor. Decide whether you need repair, envelope control, continuous harmonics, time/space, or instrument generation; then compare the closest peers.

1. Equalizers: tone, surgery, and broad-stroke color

The fastest choice is by curve behavior: modern/dynamic for repair, active proportional-Q for focus, passive parallel curves for weight and sheen, and console EQs for fast musical commitment.

Reference 1956 · UNLICENSED ID: Manley Enhanced Pultec EQ; Pultec EQP-1A descendant

Best at — Huge, soft shelves and the simultaneous boost/attenuation “Pultec trick,” with audible tube-stage wear.

Choose it when — You want more patina and nonlinear personality than Tube-Tech PE 1C, or M/S widening without leaving the plug-in.

Get more from it — Drive the input before boosting; offset low boost with low attenuation; in M/S, add air to Side while keeping sub boosts in Mid.

Genre move — On boom-bap drums, boost/attenuate 60 Hz for a round kick that still leaves room for bass.

Reference 1998 · UNLICENSED ID: Manley Massive Passive

Best at — Four parallel passive bands, luxurious broad curves, tube depth, and mix-bus polish.

Choose it when — You want silkier, more overlapping broad bands than Curve Bender, and less explicit console bite than Trident/API-style EQ.

Get more from it — Start with 0.5–1.5 dB moves; use bandwidth before gain; compare level-matched because the tube stage can make “louder” seem “better.”

Genre move — On melodic-house premaster, lift 27 kHz gently and trim low-mid haze with a broad bell.

Chandler Limited Curve Bender · OFFICIAL MODEL: Chandler Limited EMI TG12345 Curve Bender

Best at — Silky mastering top, focused mids, tight lows, and broad germanium/inductor color derived from the Abbey Road TG desk.

Choose it when — Choose it over Reference 1998 for a firmer EMI shape and over 1956 for four-band contour; use modern Equalizers for surgical work.

Get more from it — Keep X1 for 0.5 dB mastering precision; use X1.5 for larger mix moves; set high/low cuts before adding broad boosts.

Genre move — On psychedelic-soul master, add low weight and open the top while keeping the midrange vivid.

Equalizers · SOFTUBE ORIGINAL: modern dynamic EQ plus optional vintage-EQ slot

Best at — Precise static or dynamic correction, M/S and L/R work, and combining surgical control with a vintage curve in one view.

Choose it when — You need problem-solving or moving resonances; pick a modeled EQ instead when its restrictions help you decide faster.

Get more from it — Use external sidechain for kick-versus-bass space; audition detector bands soloed; load a vintage EQ only after the corrective bands are stable.

Genre move — On reggaeton, duck bass around the kick fundamental and tame vocal harshness only on loud syllables.

Core Vintage Equalizers — Single · SOFTUBE ORIGINAL: passive/parallel-style EQ

Best at — Fast broad shaping with low CPU and a forgiving, phase-rich feel.

Choose it when — You need one broad tone move, not the deeper dual-band interaction or a named hardware personality.

Get more from it — Sweep while boosted, then halve the gain; use it early across many channels for coherent coloration.

Genre move — On indie folk, give acoustic guitar a broad upper-mid lift without making pick noise clinical.

Core Vintage Equalizers — Dual · SOFTUBE ORIGINAL: dual passive/parallel-style EQ

Best at — Two interacting broad bands for weight plus presence.

Choose it when — You need a little more contour than Single but less complexity than Reference 1998.

Get more from it — Use one band to establish body and the other to restore articulation; avoid stacking two large boosts at adjacent frequencies.

Genre move — On neo-soul keys, add low-mid warmth and a high, wide gloss.

British Class A Equalizer · LICENSED ARCHETYPE: Neve 1073-era Class-A console EQ/filter lineage

Choose it when — Pick it over American Class A for density and weight; pick Trident for more aggressive upper mids.

Get more from it — Push the input/drive first, then EQ into the harmonics; use the high-pass filter to stop transformer color from bloating the sub range.

Choose it when — Pick it over API-style EQ when electric guitars need a vivid, slightly unruly edge; choose Neve-style for thicker lows.

Get more from it — Try boost and cut in neighboring bands; the phase interaction is part of the sound; drive it lightly before EQ.

2. Dynamics: compressors, limiters, gates, and transient control

Think in envelopes: FET for speed and edge, opto for program-following smoothness, VCA for punch/control, diode-bridge for density, vari-mu for glue, and digital Weiss tools for transparent precision.

FET Compressor Mk II · UNLICENSED ID: UREI/Universal Audio 1176 lineage

Best at — Fast peak control, audible urgency, parallel bite, and classic all-buttons-style aggression.

Choose it when — Choose it over OPTO for consonants and drum transients; choose VCA for cleaner punch or Bus Processor for whole-mix cohesion.

Get more from it — For the classic 1176 trick, engage British Mode, use a slower attack and fast release, drive Input until it pumps, then blend with dry. Try 1:1 when you want its color without gain reduction.

Genre move — On rap vocal, catch peaks before an opto stage and add edge without flattening every word.

OPTO Compressor · UNLICENSED ARCHETYPE: Teletronix LA-2A-style optical leveling

Best at — Smooth, slow, program-dependent leveling and low-fatigue vocal/bass control.

Choose it when — Pick it after FET for average-level control; choose TLA-100A for weightier, forgiving tube/optical leveling with simple timing choices, or CL 1B for detailed attack/release shaping.

Get more from it — Drive the input until it “breathes,” not clamps; use sidechain emphasis sparingly; chain after a fast catcher.

Genre move — On soul vocal, let FET catch plosives and OPTO hold the performance forward.

VCA Compressor · UNLICENSED ARCHETYPE: dbx 160/160 VU-style feed-forward VCA

Best at — Compact punch, firm bass control, and snare/kick impact.

Choose it when — Choose it over FET when you want less fizz and a more contained “thwack”; use Bus Processor for stereo glue.

Get more from it — Use medium attack to preserve the click, quick release to reset by the next hit, and parallel mix for body.

Genre move — On disco bass, clamp inconsistent notes while keeping a percussive front edge.

Genre move — On pop-rock mix bus, keep kick transients intact while pulling guitars and vocals into one frame.

3. Channel strips and integrated workflows

These combine compatible stages and encourage fast, console-like decisions. Pick by attitude first, then by feature depth.

Get more from it — Set input/drive across the session first, then filters, dynamics, and EQ; use the same gain-staging convention on every channel.

Genre recipes: choosing a chain for a reason

These are starting architectures, not fixed presets. The useful idea is the division of labor between stages.

1. Melodic house: wide but anchored drop

Model 84 → Harmonics → Stereo Delay; sidechain the delay/reverb, not the synth’s dry center. Bus Processor glues at 1–2 dB GR; Reference 1998 adds a very small air shelf.

2. Modern pop vocal

Vocal Tuner → FET Mk II (peaks) → CL 1B Mk II (level) → Equalizers dynamic harshness band → Atlantis Dual Chambers. Split de-essing around bright EQ if needed.

3. Boom-bap drum bus

Reference 1956 low boost/attenuation → Dyna-mite Slam in parallel → Dirty Tape at low instability. Keep kick fundamental out of the slam detector.

4. Progressive-metal guitars

TUBE Overdrive → ENGL Savage Mk II → two complementary cabs. Quad-track with less gain than a single take; use Celestion Speaker Shaper before surgical EQ.

5. Dub-techno chord

Model 72/84 → Tape Echoes → Spring Reverb. Print a feedback ride, then edit the performance; high-pass the feedback path so the kick remains immovable.

6. Cinematic hybrid score

Model 77 → Model 77 Reverb for character, then TSAR-1 as shared hall. Use Bus Processor 670 lightly across synth/strings, with M/S control preserving the center.

7. Neo-soul rhythm section

American Class A on drums for snap; Summit Grand Channel on vocal; Bass Standard Line V8 blended with DI; Reference 1998 for broad mix-bus warmth.

8. Jazz acoustic master

Weiss EQ1 for room-mode correction → Tube-Tech SMC 2B only where the spectrum moves → Weiss DS1-MK3 for rare peaks. Keep every step level-matched and conservative.

9. Industrial sound design

Buchla 259e → Doepfer BBD → OTO Biscuit → Wasted Space. Record long automation passes, then curate moments rather than running the whole chain statically.

10. Country clean guitar

Pacific Silver → light VCA compression → Tube Delay slap → TSAR-1R room. Use the amp’s headroom and mic position for brightness before reaching for EQ.

11. Reggaeton low end

Monoment Bass with mono sub → Equalizers dynamic sidechain from kick → Clipper on the drum/bass bus. Use a range-limited band rather than full-band pumping.

12. Ambient guitar

Custom 100W clean → Fix Doubler → Mutable Clouds in Modular → Dimensions Random Hall. Keep a dry or short-room center so the performance remains locatable.
